Every level of awareness

I place each idea on Eugene Schwartz's five stages before I write a word. The stage decides the hook, how much I explain, and where the offer goes.

STAGE 1Unaware
Lead with a feeling or a story they already live. The product shows up late.
A comeback story: someone who sold their childhood binder opens one pack on their phone.
STAGE 2Problem aware
Name the frustration in their words, then show the way out.
Sold-out shelves and scalped releases, told through one collector's empty-handed weekend.
STAGE 3Solution aware
Explain how it works, in one plain sentence and three steps.
"Pick a pack, rip it, keep it or sell it back": three real app screens.
STAGE 4Product aware
Remove the last doubt with proof: reviews, real pulls, delivery.
A group chat that calls it fake, then watches the live reveal.
STAGE 5Most aware
Get out of the way. The offer is the headline.
"Code crown gets you a free pack" in the first second of Slow and Steady.

Theory, applied

I keep an 85-theory working binder for the account. These are the ones that shape most briefs.

Narrative transportation

One hero, one want, cause and effect. Absorbed viewers argue back less.

Peak-end rule

The reveal is the peak and the brand is on screen there. The offer is the second peak.

Curiosity gap

Hooks open a specific, closeable question and pay it off inside the ad.

Pattern interrupt

When the feed is loud, the quiet ad stops the thumb. Contrast beats volume.

Awareness × sophistication

The market has heard the big claims, so the angle has to be new, not louder.

Means-end laddering

Feature → benefit → the value it serves, so the copy speaks to the value.

Social proof

Reviews, reactions and real pulls carry more weight than our own claims.

One-variable testing

Each pair changes one thing (the offer, or the hook) so the result means something.

Ads with real spend

Every piece here ran in paid social. Sorted by lifetime spend; the two with case studies show how they were made.
