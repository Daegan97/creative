Slow and Steady

In a feed full of shouting rip reactions, the ad that scaled furthest was read by the sleepiest narrator I could cast. It became my most-scaled creative.

Format
9:16 video · 19s cut
Lifetime spend
$24K+
Offer
Free pack with a referral code
Awareness stage
Most aware: offer in second one
My role
Concept, script, VO casting, brief, footage pull
Edit
Team video editor, cut from my brief

The problem

Every competitor in the category runs the same ad: a creator screaming at a pull, a dollar figure in yellow, "INSANE!!!" in the caption. It works for a while, then the feed learns to scroll past it. We needed a short, cheap-to-edit ad that put the offer in the first second and still stopped the thumb.

The insight

  • The wait is the product. Our customer research showed the best moment of a rip is the build before the reveal, not the number after it. A slow voice turns the build into the whole ad.
  • Contrast beats volume. When everything around it is loud, a whisper is the pattern interrupt.
  • Let the pull write the joke. I searched our own footage for a reveal that fit the voice and found a Slowpoke & Psyduck slab, graded a 10. The card pays off the concept on its own.

The brief

One page, built so the editor could cut it in a single pass:

VO 50 · "Slow and Steady"9:16 · 0:28 brief · free pack, code in VO
VoiceMale 30s, extremely slow, sleepy deadpan. Every line delivered like he's about to fall asleep mid-word.
TimeFootageOn screenVO
0:00–0:05Sapphire pack screenWord-pop captions"Code… crown… gets you… a free pack."
0:05–0:10Pack select, rip, card backs"pack" · "Rip""A Sapphire pack… first… rip…"
0:10–0:14Card back climbs to gold"Poke""Slow…poke…"
0:14–0:22Slowpoke & Psyduck slab, value held 3–5sReal value from the app screen"…and Psyduck. Graded a 10. Seventeen hundred fifty-five dollars."
0:22–0:28End cardFree pack · use code · store badges"Free pack. Code crown. Take your time."

What shipped with it: the clip pre-cut from our own screen recording, the generated voiceover as an MP3 laid at 0:00, an SRT of the captions, and flags (cover the account balance, keep the offer to one code).

The cut

The editor kept the pacing and set one word on screen per beat, so the captions slow down with the voice.

Why it worked

  • Offer first, offer last. The code is the first word and the last line, so even a two-second viewer leaves with it.
  • One peak, brand on screen. The reveal is the only big moment, and the app's own price screen is what's on screen when it lands.
  • Cheap to make, easy to iterate. One screen recording, one voice, word captions. The editor turned it around fast, and the concept can be re-cast with any "slow" pull.

What I'd test next

Keep the voice and swap one variable at a time: a different pull with a pun to match, a different offer in the same slot, and a 10-second cut that starts on the reveal.
